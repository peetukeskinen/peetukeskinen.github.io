EU debt rule calculator

Under the reformed EU fiscal rules, a member state's required fiscal effort comes out of a debt sustainability analysis rather than a single deficit number. This page runs that analysis for Finland in your browser. Move a control and watch the debt trajectory bend, the net expenditure growth ceilings change, and the binding rule switch.

An independent replication for teaching and illustration, built from my open MATLAB tool. It is not an official calculation of the European Commission or of the National Audit Office of Finland, and it carries no institutional endorsement.

      How it works

      The net expenditure path is the operational indicator used to monitor a country's fiscal plan. The chart above shows this calculator's annual nominal growth ceilings, including any deficit benchmark or resilience safeguard top-ups. The structural primary balance (SPB) adjustment is an intermediate step in deriving those ceilings. These illustrative results are not a Council-endorsed plan.

      The calculator searches, in steps of 0.01 up to 2.00 pp of GDP a year, for the smallest constant annual improvement in the structural primary balance that passes the DSA tests and the debt safeguard. The deficit benchmark and the resilience safeguard then raise individual years on top of that, as the year-by-year table shows; the debt chart is drawn at the constant adjustment. If a rule cannot be met by any adjustment in that range, the page says so instead of reporting a number. Debt is projected forward with the Commission's own accounting: the implicit interest rate is rebuilt each year from the maturity structure of the debt, adjustment costs output through a fiscal multiplier, the output gap closes gradually, and ageing costs are carried through the review period.

      The grey line on the chart is the reference trajectory — the Commission's baseline assumptions, or a scenario you have pinned — so that any change is seen against something that stays still. The bars below the chart show what each rule would require on its own; the longest of the DSA and debt-safeguard bars is the rule that sets the underlying constant adjustment, and the two floor bars only top up the years shown beside them.

      The criteria are:

      • Deterministic DSA. Debt has to fall in every year of the ten-year review period in four scenarios: the baseline, a permanent slippage of the structural primary balance, permanently worse interest rates and growth, and a temporary episode of market stress. The baseline also has to keep the headline deficit below 3% of GDP.
      • Stochastic DSA. Shocks to growth, the primary balance and interest rates are simulated for the five years after the plan — the shaded fan. Debt has to be lower after those five years than at the end of the plan in the chosen share of simulated paths.
      • Debt sustainability safeguard. Debt has to fall on average by at least 1 pp of GDP a year while it is above 90% of GDP, or 0.5 pp while it is between 60% and 90%. On the chart this is the dashed slope across the plan window that the path has to beat.
      • Deficit benchmark. At least 0.5 pp of adjustment a year while the deficit is above 3% of GDP.
      • Deficit resilience safeguard. At least 0.4 pp a year (0.25 pp under a seven-year plan) until the structural balance reaches −1.5% of GDP.

      The requirement is then expressed the way the rules are actually written: as a ceiling on the growth of net primary expenditure, which is what a country's plan commits to. The year-by-year numbers are in the table above.

      Data and limitations

      • Data: European Commission prior guidance, 2024, for Finland, with the plan starting in 2025. Changing the controls moves the assumptions away from that vintage; it does not load a newer forecast.
      • The simulated shocks are a fixed set of 1,000 draws, generated once. The normal option draws them from the historical covariance of quarterly growth, balance and interest rate innovations, with outliers beyond three standard deviations clipped first as in the MATLAB tool; the bootstrap option resamples two-year blocks of the annual historical deviations. Both sets are fixed, so everyone loading the page sees the same result. They are not drawn afresh in the browser, and they are not the MATLAB tool's own draws.
      • The model reproduces the published MATLAB tool, including its simplifications. It applies the deficit benchmark in structural primary terms throughout, rather than switching to structural balance terms partway through, and it takes the safeguard's 0.5 or 1 pp band from the 2023 debt ratio rather than from debt at the start of the plan.
      • Results are illustrative. The Commission's own calculation uses the full country data, its own forecast vintage and judgement that no replication reproduces exactly.

      The method follows the Commission's Debt Sustainability Monitor 2023, and the code benefited from the Python implementation by Darvas et al. (2023). The MATLAB version this page replicates is DSA 5.1.

EU debt rule calculator

Under the reformed EU fiscal rules, a member state's required fiscal effort comes out of a debt sustainability analysis rather than a single deficit number. This page runs that analysis for Finland in your browser: change the assumptions and see how much annual consolidation the rules demand, and which rule it is that binds.

An independent replication for teaching and illustration, built from my open MATLAB tool. It is not an official calculation of the European Commission or of the National Audit Office of Finland, and it carries no institutional endorsement.

    How it works

    The calculator searches for the smallest constant annual improvement in the structural primary balance that satisfies every criterion in force. Debt is projected forward with the Commission's own accounting: the implicit interest rate is rebuilt each year from the maturity structure of the debt, consolidation costs output through a fiscal multiplier, the output gap closes gradually, and ageing costs are carried through the review period.

    The criteria are:

    • Deterministic DSA. Debt has to fall in every year of the ten-year review period in four scenarios: the baseline, a permanent slippage of the structural primary balance, permanently worse interest rates and growth, and a temporary episode of market stress. The baseline also has to keep the headline deficit below 3% of GDP.
    • Stochastic DSA. Shocks to growth, the primary balance and interest rates are simulated for the five years after the plan. Debt has to be lower after those five years than at the end of the plan in the chosen share of simulated paths.
    • Debt sustainability safeguard. Debt has to fall on average by at least 1 pp of GDP a year while it is above 90% of GDP, or 0.5 pp while it is between 60% and 90%.
    • Deficit benchmark. At least 0.5 pp of adjustment a year while the deficit is above 3% of GDP.
    • Deficit resilience safeguard. At least 0.4 pp a year (0.25 pp under a seven-year plan) until the structural balance reaches −1.5% of GDP.

    The requirement is then expressed the way the rules are actually written: as a ceiling on the growth of net primary expenditure, which is what a country's plan commits to.

    Data and limitations

    • Data: European Commission prior guidance, 2024, for Finland, with the plan starting in 2025. Changing the sliders moves the assumptions away from that vintage; it does not load a newer forecast.
    • The simulated shocks are a fixed set of 1,000 draws, generated once from the historical covariance of quarterly growth, balance and interest rate innovations, so everyone loading the page sees the same result. They are not drawn afresh in the browser, and they are not the MATLAB tool's own draws.
    • The model reproduces the published MATLAB tool, including its simplifications. It applies the deficit benchmark in structural primary terms throughout, rather than switching to structural balance terms partway through, and it takes the safeguard's debt band from the starting debt ratio.
    • Results are illustrative. The Commission's own calculation uses the full country data, its own forecast vintage and judgement that no replication reproduces exactly.

    The method follows the Commission's Debt Sustainability Monitor 2023, and the code benefited from the Python implementation by Darvas et al. (2023). The MATLAB version this page replicates is DSA 5.1.

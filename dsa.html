      What the rules ask for

      EU countries agree a fiscal plan with the Commission. The plan caps how fast public spending may grow, and the cap has to be tight enough to put debt on a falling path.

      How tight is worked out by projecting the debt ratio forward under several scenarios. The budget has to improve by enough each year that debt falls and keeps falling. That yearly improvement is the number at the top of the page.

      Several rules apply at once and the strictest one sets the requirement. The bars under the chart show what each rule asks for on its own. Two of the rules are checked every year and can push a single year higher than the rest.

      The rules

      • Debt has to fall in every one of the ten years after the plan, in four scenarios: the plan as written, weaker discipline afterwards, higher rates with lower growth, and a bout of market stress.
      • It has to be falling five years after the plan in most of 1,000 simulations of bad and good luck.
      • It has to fall during the plan as well: 0.5 points of GDP a year between 60% and 90% debt, 1 point above 90%. For Finland this is usually the rule that bites.
      • If last year's deficit was above 3% of GDP, this year needs at least 0.5 points of tightening.
      • Until the structural deficit is below 1.5%, each year needs at least 0.4 points, or 0.25 in a seven-year plan.

      Where the numbers come from

      The data is the Commission's 2024 guidance for Finland (European Commission prior guidance, 2024), with the plan starting in 2025. The method follows the Commission's Debt Sustainability Monitor. Euro amounts start from Finland's 2023 GDP of €273 bn (Eurostat) and grow with the projection.

      This is a browser version of my MATLAB tool. At the settings it opens with, it gives the same answers as the ones published in the National Audit Office's fiscal policy monitoring report 2024: 0.76 points a year over seven years, about 0.3 without the debt safeguard, and a spending cap of about 1.5%.

      Worth knowing

      • This is a personal project. It is not an official calculation and no institution stands behind it. Finland's independent fiscal institution is the Economic Policy Council.
      • Moving a control does not load a new forecast. It asks what the rules would require if that assumption held.
      • The multiplier and the inflation path are assumptions rather than measurements. The 2024 monitoring report argued that both deserve more work, which is why they are on the controls.
      • The simulations are a fixed set of 1,000 draws, so everyone sees the same fan and a reload does not change the answer.
      • The model copies the published MATLAB code, including its simplifications. It applies the deficit benchmark in structural primary terms throughout, and takes the safeguard's band from the 2023 debt ratio.
